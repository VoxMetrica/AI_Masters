# Deepfake detection paper review

[Read the review](review.pdf) — a five-page critical evaluation of *DeepFake Detection Based on Discrepancies Between Faces and Their Context* by Nirkin and colleagues, prepared for Foundations and Frontiers coursework.

The review explains the face-versus-context idea and model architecture, examines the reported benchmark evidence and discusses generalisation, occlusion, fairness assumptions and the handling of face reenactment.

This is a literature-review deliverable. The tables and architecture figure are explicitly reproduced from the reviewed paper and attributed in the document. References to “Ours” inside reproduced tables refer to that paper's authors. No model implementation, independent training run or reproduced benchmark is claimed here.

The PDF preserves the original coursework text and bibliography. Its literature coverage reflects the assignment period. One terminology caveat: Table 1 and parts of the prose use “accuracy” broadly, while the footnote identifies the metric as frame-level ROC AUC; ROC AUC should not be interpreted as thresholded classification accuracy. Further claims about robustness and fairness are arguments to investigate, not experiments performed in this project.
